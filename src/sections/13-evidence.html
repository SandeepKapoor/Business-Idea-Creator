Appendix · Evidence ledger

What every number in this document actually rests on.

Read this before you act on any figure. The document mixes three different kinds of claim and they do not deserve equal trust. Anything marked judgment is me reasoning, not me knowing.

Format base prices
9 of 22 are anchored to published market figures: Indian bootcamp fees (₹10k–1.5L; GrowthSchool ₹50–70k), freelance UX hourly in India (₹1,500–4,000), specialist corporate training per day (₹30,000–1.5L), study-abroad packages (₹30k–1.5L), fractional CXO retainers in India (CMO ₹1.5–4L growth-stage), global paid-community pricing ($29–49/mo). The remaining 13 formats — retreats, card decks, awards, directories, masterminds, study tours — have no published India benchmark I could find, and Part 9 says so on the card.

Payer multipliers
Entirely a heuristic: employer budgets tolerate 3–5× consumer prices. I found no dataset measuring this for design training specifically. The one exception is expert networks, where published expert-pay bands ($400–600/hr mid-level) let me correct the multiple downward.

The 8 scoring criteria
Ordinal judgments, not measurements. Three of the eight — distribution, founder fit, energy — are guesses about you specifically. I know two facts about you: Boeing, and Politecnico di Milano. Everything else about your network, credibility and temperament is inferred and should be overridden.

Verdict bands (32 / 29)
Now anchored to the measured distribution rather than picked by feel. I scored all 193,600 combinations: after removing gate failures, broken combinations and asset-only cases, the remaining totals have median 27, p75 = 29, p96 = 32, max 37. So BUILD ≥ 32 is the top ~4% and WORTH TESTING ≥ 29 is the top quartile. The old TEST floor of 26 sat below the median and passed 47% of everything. Still: the hard gates are the load-bearing part. The total is a sorting aid, not a truth claim.

Where I disagree with my own engine
Worth knowing, because it shows the engine's limits. #78 India→global placement is my 5th-ranked pick but scores 28 — WEAK — because it has distribution 2 and speed 2. I still rank it highly on structural grounds (a labour-market mispricing needs no marketing argument) that the eight criteria do not represent. #37 India's Got Designers goes the other way: the engine rates it 26 while my hand score is 21 with a gate failure, because "nobody pays to be judged" is not encoded in the axes. When the engine and the argued judgment differ, the argument is the more considered of the two — but neither is evidence.

The 7 twists
Seven ways to build the same idea, each with score changes and a price multiplier I set by hand. Which way each number moves is sound — going narrow raises what people will pay, charging a lot lowers it, handing delivery to others trades speed for size. How far it moves is invented.

The 8 kinds of work
Pulled out of the 112 ideas in this bank, not invented — each one names the ideas that already are it. A kind of work had to turn up in three or more formats, with no single format holding more than 60% of it, or it was cut for being the format axis under a new name. Which idea belongs to which kind of work is my reading. Where the bank has no example at a format, the page marks it J and says so. Run npm run premise for the working.

Corrections made in this audit
Five claims were wrong and are now fixed in place rather than quietly deleted: (1) the pricing model was calibrated against my own invented figures — rebuilt on published data; (2) workshops priced at ₹5.5L/day, above the market ceiling — base cut 4×; (3) "enterprise pays 1.6×" was fabricated — no such figure exists; (4) expert-network rate implied a C-suite band for a mid-career designer; (5) the founder-fit stage described engine guesses as "your own answers". One accessibility defect too: the score colour ramp failed its contrast floor in light mode and was auto-flipped for dark. Both ramps are now separately validated.

What no model here can tell you
Whether anyone will pay. Every score, price and verdict is a prior. The only instrument in this document that produces evidence is the two-week sprint in Part 9 — twenty interviews about past behaviour, then taking real money for something that does not exist yet. Treat the rest as a way to decide what to test first, and nothing more.
