10 · My position

Pure divergence without judgment is homework, not help. So — the five strongest, and why.

The systemic mistake almost everyone in this market makes

They sell craft (Figma, UI, visual polish) to individuals who can't afford it, in a marketing-spend war, teaching the exact layer of the work that AI is currently automating.

Four compounding errors in one business model. And it's visible right inside this bank: the ideas that fail the gates are almost all "teach craft to a designer," while the ideas that clear every gate almost all invoice an employer for judgment. The second mistake is subtler and you're at risk of it specifically: mistaking an audience for a business. A show, a podcast and a community are distribution assets. They are not revenue. Build the invoice first, then build the audience that feeds it — the reverse order takes three years and usually stalls.

Dead ground — don't walk here

Generic UI/UX Figma bootcamps for Indian individuals
Fully commoditised. GrowthSchool and DesignBoat have brand, capital and paid acquisition; the fee has one direction to go. You'd be competing on ad spend against companies doing ₹5–25 crore of revenue, with no structural advantage. Do not enter.
Self-paced courses on Udemy
A race to ₹449 on a platform that owns the customer, controls discounting, and where 85–95% of buyers never finish. Zero brand equity accrues to you. It's not a business, it's a lottery ticket with homework.
Another AI-tool / design-tool directory
Every incumbent is affiliate-farmed, untested and SEO-decaying, and thousands exist. There is exactly one non-dead version — #89, where you actually test the tools and publish honest comparative output — and its moat is your credibility, not the database. If you're not willing to be the one who says a popular tool is bad, skip it.
"Design thinking" workshops sold on personality
Sold to individuals, this is dead — an over-supplied, discredited category. Sold to a corporate L&D budget with a named business outcome attached (#29, #110), the same four hours is worth ₹4–8 lakh. Identical work. The payer is the entire difference. Which is Observation 1 again, and it's the thing to take away from this whole document.

One concrete action, this week

Twenty conversations in seven days. Ten and ten. No product, no deck, no landing page.

Ten design or engineering managers at Indian GCCs — Boeing, Airbus, Siemens, Shell, Mercedes-Benz R&D, Bosch. You already have warm paths to most of them. One question: "What did your team's training budget get spent on last year, who decided, and what did you wish existed instead?" You are listening for a budget line and an unmet need, nothing else.

Ten mid-level Indian designers — 3–6 years in. One question: "What's the last thing you paid money for to get better at design, how much was it, and did you finish it?"

In parallel, ship issue #1 of the enterprise UX newsletter (#67) this weekend. One teardown of one boring, high-stakes interface. It costs you a Saturday, it starts compounding immediately, it qualifies leads for #23, and it's the cheapest proof of the whole thesis. Do not build anything else until those 20 conversations are done.

Then come back and say "go deep on 23" (or any number) — market sizing, named competitors, pricing ladder, unit economics, a 30-day MVP, the top three risks and the kill criterion. Or "more in cluster F" for 15 fresh ones. Or "score these 8" for any set you like better than mine.
