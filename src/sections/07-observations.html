5 · What the bank reveals

Patterns I noticed while generating. This is the part worth more than any single idea.

Observation 1 · the big one
Every strong idea invoices a company. Almost none invoice a designer.
Individual Indian designers are the worst customer in this market: poorest, most discount-driven, highest churn, and they compare you on price against a ₹449 Udemy course. Their employers have allocated, expiring L&D budget and no price sensitivity. Identical delivery, 3–5× the price, one-tenth the marketing. Your entire instinct list (course, podcast, community) points at the individual. That's the single most expensive assumption in your thinking.
Observation 2
Everyone competes on content. Nobody competes on accountability or proof.
Content is now free and infinitely generated. What isn't free is finishing: peer-reviewed research puts median completion across 221 MOOCs at 12.6% (MIT measured 3.13% on its own 2017–18 courses), and Harvard found 3.4× higher dropout without peer or coach accountability. Note what I am not claiming: the widely-quoted "85–95% for cohorts" comes from companies that sell cohort software, and their own numbers contradict each other — one of them reports 64.2% vs 48.2% for the same comparison. The direction is solid; the magnitude is marketing. #41 deletes the curriculum entirely and sells only the container. Downstream of that sits the highest-margin position in any education market: assessment and certification (#52), which survives when teaching commoditises.
Observation 3
Your advantage is complexity and strategy — not design teaching.
Anyone can teach Figma; it's copyable in a weekend, which is why that market is a marketing-spend war. Safety-critical, regulated, dense-data design plus a European strategic-design method cannot be copied in six months. Every single Indian design educator sits on the copyable side of that line. You can stand on the other side of it on day one — and you're the only one who can.
Observation 4
Your favourite ideas are distribution, not businesses.
India's Got Designers, the roast show, the gaming podcast (#37, #68, #69) — these are excellent top-of-funnel and terrible P&Ls. Ad and sponsor revenue at your likely scale is ₹20–80k a month for enormous production effort, and it's location-dependent the moment it involves a stage. Run them as the marketing for something that invoices. Never as the revenue line. See the scorecard — #37 fails the willingness-to-pay gate outright.
Observation 5 · expiry dates
Two of your best positions decay if you wait.
#104 (documenting your own corporate exit) is worth the most while you are mid-exit — that credibility window is about 9 months wide and then you're just another person selling a course about quitting. Cluster I (AI repositioning, agentic UX, design QA for AI-generated interfaces) is unclaimed right now and will have ten credible teachers by mid-2027. Most of this bank keeps. These two don't.
Observation 6
"Live anywhere" quietly eliminates about a third of the bank.
You said location independence is the actual goal. That kills the retreats, the stage shows, the offline coaching, the pop-up museum, and most of the workshop economics — or at least makes them a once-a-quarter luxury rather than the engine. It strongly favours async critique (#43), retainers (#53, #108, #112), licensing (#51), certification (#52) and anything a company pays for over video. Weigh this axis explicitly; it conflicts with several ideas you're emotionally drawn to.
