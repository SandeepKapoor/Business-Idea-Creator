Become a Founder

A design-led founder track: designers who want to start their own company, taught the parts design school never covers — business model, pricing, fundraising narrative, and shipping v1 without a team.

ProblemCustomer Value propModel & pricing Go-to-marketCompetition Risks90-day roadmap Revenue skeleton

Problem & evidence

Designers who want to start a company have design skill and no business vocabulary — they can make the product but not the pitch deck, the pricing model, or the case for why a customer should pay. The bank's own evidence ledger lists #113 Business Thinking for Designers and #114 Start Your First Company as a Designer as exactly this gap, added after the main research pass with no market sizing behind either. Idea #113/#114, evidence ledger — flagged there as the weakest-evidenced pair on the site; treat this problem statement as directionally right, not measured.

Target customer

Mid-career designers (3-8 years in) who already have a product idea or a strong opinion about one, currently employed, and either quietly building nights-and-weekends or about to quit. Distinct from the bootcamp buyer — this person can already design; what they lack is the founder's toolkit.

Value proposition

Everything a design degree skips: naming the business model before the product, pricing without guessing, telling a fundraising story a non-designer investor will believe, and shipping a v1 alone. Taught by someone who has actually sat inside a company (Boeing) and a strategic-design program (Politecnico di Milano) rather than a generic "how to start a startup" course. Founder facts: evidence ledger, idea #17-final.html §evidence ledger.

Business model & pricing

Cohort-based, 6-8 weeks, individual-paid — the one idea in this set of six where the individual is correctly the payer, because the buyer is pre-revenue and there is no employer to bill yet. Anchor price against Indian bootcamp fees (₹10k-1.5L range) rather than inventing a new number. Pricing anchor: evidence ledger, "format base prices". Starting price: ₹35,000-45,000 per seat, cohort of 15-20. judgment

Go-to-market

Direct outreach into design communities and Slack/Discord groups where designers already discuss "should I quit and build my own thing" — a warmer, cheaper channel than paid ads. A free 90-minute "should you actually start a company" session as the funnel, same shape as the Strategy Brief's validation-gate design (free session, then paid). Funnel pattern: strategy brief, §The Next Six Weeks.

Competition & differentiation

Generic startup accelerators and YC-style content teach the business side but assume the founder already knows how to build; design bootcamps teach the craft but never touch business model or fundraising. The gap between the two is this idea's whole space — narrow, but currently empty for designers specifically.

90-day roadmap

Weeks 1-310 conversations with designers who considered quitting to found something. Ask what stopped them — money, knowledge, or nerve.
Weeks 4-6Run one free 90-minute session for 5-8 designers. Watch which parts (pricing? pitch? shipping alone?) generate the most questions.

Design for Enterprise UX

Enterprise-grade UX training for GCC and enterprise design teams, sold to the L&D budget, taught from real safety-critical/complex-systems experience rather than a repackaged consumer bootcamp.

ProblemCustomer Value propModel & pricing Go-to-marketCompetition Risks90-day roadmap Revenue skeleton

Problem & evidence

This is the highest-scoring candidate from the site's own independent four-stage re-run: GCCs have an allocated, expiring L&D budget and are judged on outcomes, not price, but no India-based teacher has actually shipped safety-critical or complex-systems work. Final tab, Stage 1 user segments + Stage 3/4 — final score 6.85, highest of all twelve candidates generated there.

Target customer

The L&D or engineering-training budget owner inside an Indian GCC — Boeing, Airbus, Siemens, Shell, Mercedes-Benz R&D, Bosch and similar. idea #12-position.html — named GCC list with an existing warm-path claim through the founder's Boeing network.

Value proposition

Training taught from inside safety-critical, complex-systems delivery — not a weekend-copyable Figma course. The uncopyable edge is structural: craft is copyable, six months of Figma tutorials is not a moat, but shipping design decisions inside a regulated, high-consequence environment is. Final tab, Stage 1, "Recurring problems" #3.

Business model & pricing

Per-cohort fee billed to the GCC's training budget. Anchor: specialist corporate training in India runs ₹30,000-1.5L/day. Evidence ledger, "format base prices". Starting anchor: ₹30,000-1.5L/day, multi-day cohort format. Final tab, Stage 4 comparison table.

Go-to-market

Warm-intro conversations through the founder's own employer network — no cold outreach, no paid acquisition needed to get the first conversations. Final tab, Stage 3 hard-filter table: "Pass — founder's own employer network".

Competition & differentiation

Generic consumer UX bootcamps and global training vendors compete on price and reach, not on complex-systems credibility. No other Indian educator currently sits on the safety-critical/complex-systems side of that line, per the site's own evidence ledger — the founder's Boeing and Polimi background are the only two verified facts about them anywhere on the site. Evidence ledger, "The 8 scoring criteria".

Risks & open questions

QuestionStatus

Does the 3-5× employer-vs-individual payer multiplier actually hold for design training specifically, or only as a heuristic?CRITICAL — Needs Validation
Which channel reaches the L&D buyer first — warm intro, cold outreach, or content?Minor — Needs Validation

90-day roadmap

Weeks 1-25-10 warm-intro conversations with GCC L&D or engineering-training owners. Ask what last year's training budget was spent on and why.
Weeks 3-5Draft a one-day pilot curriculum from the strongest recurring theme in those conversations.
Weeks 6-9Sell one paid pilot day, real invoice, to the warmest of the contacted GCCs.
Weeks 10-13Deliver the pilot, collect a structured outcome review, and quote the next GCC at the tested price.

Revenue skeleton

One 2-day cohort at ₹75,000/day × 2 = ₹1.5L per engagement. At 8 GCC engagements/year, ~₹12L/year run solo; scales by adding facilitators once demand exceeds one person's calendar. judgment — untested price point

Design Thinking for Students & Young People

Design-thinking literacy for school and college students, sold to parents or institutions rather than to teenagers directly — the same "wrong payer" correction applied to a younger segment.

ProblemCustomer Value propModel & pricing Go-to-marketCompetition Risks90-day roadmap Revenue skeleton

Problem & evidence

Design-thinking exposure for students is currently either absent from Indian curricula or delivered as a one-off workshop with no follow-through. The bank names this segment directly — parents of design aspirants, seasonal around entrance exams and study-abroad applications — as price-insensitive and currently unserved by any dedicated local curriculum. Final tab, Stage 1 user segments, "Parents of design aspirants / design students"; idea #32/#33/#34.

Target customer

Parents of students aged 14-19 considering a design career or a study-abroad design program, plus schools/colleges looking to add a differentiated elective. Parent is the real payer, price-insensitive, seasonal around entrance-exam and application windows. Final tab, Stage 1 — same segment description.

Value proposition

A structured design-thinking foundation before a portfolio or an application exists — framed as career and admissions preparation, not a hobby workshop, so it reads as arithmetic to a parent: this specific skill improves this specific application outcome. Strategy brief, Insight 10 — "choose a promise that is arithmetic".

Business model & pricing

Cohort-based short course (4-6 weeks), sold direct to parents or licensed to schools per-seat. Anchor: study-abroad packages price at ₹30k-1.5L; this is a lighter-touch product than a full application-coaching package, so priced below that band. Evidence ledger, "format base prices". Starting price: ₹12,000-18,000 per student, direct-to-parent. judgment

Go-to-market

Partnerships with schools/colleges as a co-branded elective, plus direct outreach to parent communities around entrance-exam season. Seasonal timing means the launch window matters more than for the other five ideas here — miss the application season and demand resets for months. judgment — no evidenced channel on this site for reaching parents specifically; the seasonality claim is evidenced, the channel is not.

Competition & differentiation

Competes with generic study-abroad consultants (application logistics, not design thinking itself) and with international design-thinking curricula not localized for Indian students. Differentiation is the same uncopyable edge as the enterprise idea, repositioned for a younger audience: real practising-designer judgment, not a licensed US curriculum reskinned.

Risks & open questions

QuestionStatus
Does design-thinking training measurably help a student's design-school application, or is that claim assumed rather than evidenced?CRITICAL — Needs Validation
Will schools license this per-seat, or does every sale have to go direct-to-parent?Needs Validation
How much does demand collapse outside the entrance-exam/application season?Minor — Needs Validation

90-day roadmap

Weeks 1-310 conversations with parents of design-track students. Ask what they've already paid for (portfolio coaching, entrance test prep) and how much.
Weeks 4-6Approach 3-5 schools/colleges about a co-branded elective slot; gauge institutional appetite versus direct-to-parent-only.
Weeks 7-10Run one free session for 8-10 students; watch whether parents ask about the next cohort unprompted.
Weeks 11-13Pre-sell the first paid cohort timed to the nearest application/entrance-exam window.

Revenue skeleton

Cohort of 20 at ₹15,000 = ₹3L per cohort. Seasonality likely caps this at 2-3 cohorts/year run solo (~₹6-9L/year) unless a school partnership adds off-season volume. judgment

Design Innovation & Transformation for SMB

Design-led innovation and transformation consulting for small and mid-size businesses that have never had internal design capability — a smaller, faster-cycle version of the enterprise engagement, sold to owners rather than L&D departments.

ProblemCustomer Value propModel & pricing Go-to-marketCompetition Risks90-day roadmap Revenue skeleton

Problem & evidence

SMBs are the "abandoned majority" the site's own strategy brief names directly: design work happens inside banks, hospitals and internal tools with almost no design maturity and no dedicated curriculum or consulting model built for their scale or budget. Strategy brief, §Twelve Families / §Opportunity map — "B2B and enterprise design... where most designers actually work, with almost no material written for them".

Target customer

SMB owners and operators (₹5-50 Cr revenue range) who compete on product/service quality but have no design function and no budget for a full-time hire — the segment between "too small to hire a designer" and "too unsophisticated to know they need one".

Value proposition

A fixed-scope design-innovation sprint (not an open-ended retainer) that gives an SMB owner a concrete transformation output — a repositioned product, a redesigned customer journey, a validated new offering — in weeks, using the same strategic-design method taught at enterprise scale but compressed for SMB budgets and timelines.

Business model & pricing

Fixed-fee project engagement, 2-4 weeks, sold direct to the owner. Anchor: fractional CXO retainers in India run ₹1.5-4L/month for growth-stage companies. Evidence ledger, "format base prices". Priced below a full fractional retainer since this is one bounded sprint, not ongoing leadership: ₹1.5-3L per sprint. judgment

Go-to-market

Direct relationship-based sales into local SMB networks (trade associations, chambers of commerce, founder communities) rather than inbound content — SMB owners are not searching for "design innovation consulting" by name, so the channel has to name their actual problem (declining margins, a stale product line) instead. judgment — no evidenced SMB acquisition channel on this site.

Competition & differentiation

Competes with generalist management consultants (McKinsey-style but no SMB reach) and freelance designers (craft only, no strategic framing). The gap is a consultant who can do both — strategic-design method plus a fixed, affordable, SMB-sized engagement — which the market currently serves only at enterprise price points or not at all.

Risks & open questions

QuestionStatus
Do SMB owners recognize "design innovation" as a category worth paying for, or does this need to be sold as something else (revenue growth, product refresh)?CRITICAL — Needs Validation
Is ₹1.5-3L per sprint affordable at typical SMB budgets, or does the price point need to be much lower?Needs Validation
What specific SMB network gives first-customer access without an existing relationship?CRITICAL — Needs Validation

90-day roadmap

Weeks 1-310 conversations with SMB owners about their last attempt at a product/service refresh — what they tried, what they paid, what failed.
Weeks 4-6Package one bounded sprint offer, framed around the strongest recurring complaint from those conversations.
Weeks 7-10Sell the first paid sprint at a discounted founding-client price, real invoice.
Weeks 11-13Deliver, document the before/after as a case study, and quote the next SMB at full price.

Revenue skeleton

4-6 sprints/year at ₹2L average = ₹8-12L/year run solo. Ceiling is calendar-bound until a second consultant is added. judgment

Design-literacy training for PMs, founders and engineers who now ship design decisions themselves with AI tools — sold to the company, so the payer is correct from the start.

Problem & evidence

Strategy brief, Group C table, "Design literacy for PMs & founders".

Target customer

A product/engineering leader whose PMs and engineers keep shipping weak design decisions, judged on design quality without ever being trained in it, with the company carrying the cost of that gap. Final tab, Stage 4 comparison table.

Enough design judgment to brief and evaluate design work correctly, without becoming a designer — a workshop or short course, not a craft bootcamp. Final tab, Stage 4 comparison table.

Direct outreach to product/engineering leaders — no audience or gatekeeper required, passes the site's own hard-filter test for first-customer reachability. Final tab, Stage 3 hard-filter table: "Pass — no audience required, direct outreach" (idea #02, same filter logic applies to #09).

Most copyable of the six ideas here — generic design-literacy training for PMs is not a hard moat, per the site's own Stage 4 comparison table. Real Simulation for Design Work in Industry

Role-play cohorts that simulate the actual mess of shipping design work — hostile stakeholders, moving deadlines, engineering pushback — the pedagogy the strategy brief's own top recommendation is built on.

ProblemCustomer Value propModel & pricing Go-to-marketCompetition Risks90-day roadmap Revenue skeleton

Problem & evidence

Design school teaches the clean version of a messy job — greenfield briefs, no politics, no moving deadlines. The strategy brief's own top-ranked recommendation, "The Real Work," is built entirely on this gap and on the claim that its pedagogy (role-play under pressure) is the differentiation, because it cannot be delivered by a recording, a PDF, or a language model. Strategy brief, §ed-b-pick, "The recommendation".

Target customer

Individual designers, most plausibly employed ones whose employer will fund it once it has proven itself on individuals first — the strategy brief's own stated growth sequence. Strategy brief, §ed-b-pick, "The growth sequence" — small group, then companies pay far more for the same five weeks.

Value proposition

Not lectures — role-play. Present to a hostile stakeholder, get torn apart, do it again; negotiate scope with an engineer who says no; scope and price the work honestly; ship a compromise with your standards intact. Strategy brief, §ed-b-pick, "How it's taught" and the five-week structure.

Business model & pricing

Live cohort, twelve people, five weeks, online, individual-paid initially, escalating to a company-paid version once proven. Strategy brief, §ed-b-pick. No published India-specific anchor for this exact format exists in the evidence ledger; nearest anchor is the cohort/bootcamp band (₹10k-1.5L). judgment — price not yet tested for this specific format

Go-to-market

No capital, no platform, no product required to test — a video call and twelve people, testable inside six weeks. Strategy brief, §ed-b-pick, "Why this one, against the alternatives".

Competition & differentiation

Almost nobody teaches surviving design, versus a crowded field that teaches design itself. The pedagogy is the moat — a recording or an AI tool cannot reproduce being torn apart in a live critique and going again. Strategy brief, §ed-b-pick.

Risks & open questions

QuestionStatus
Borrowed authority: has the founder actually survived the specific pressures being simulated, at enough depth to play every difficult role convincingly?CRITICAL — the strategy brief names this as its own open question.

Reuses the strategy brief's own validation gates rather than inventing new ones. Strategy brief, §ed-b-next.
